02 / PROJECT

From demonstration to dataset.

Contact-rich key insertion is difficult to learn without examples of motion and interaction forces. The current design aims to capture human demonstrations in a synchronized dataset for later UR5 learning and testing; sensing performance has not yet been experimentally validated.

03 / DESIGN

How we chose the collection system.

The team selected a demonstration framework first, then the handheld architecture that would make it practical.

01 / FRAMEWORK

Demonstration Framework Selection

DF-1Selected framework

Instrumented Handheld with Motorized End Effector

Selected for a favorable balance of natural manipulation, portability, direct force/torque measurement, and implementation flexibility.

Key tradeoff

Operator-induced parasitic loading can affect F/T measurements. Mechanical design, calibration, compensation, and validation must address it.

FRAMEWORKS COMPARED
  • DF-2GELLO Teleoperation
  • DF-3SpaceMouse Teleoperation
  • DF-4Vision/Pose-Only Handheld
  • DF-5Kinesthetic / Hand-Guided UR5
02 / HANDHELD

Handheld Architecture Selection

HH-1Selected architecture

One-Hand Motorized EE + Button

Balances manipulation precision, F/T load-path integrity, sensor integration, modularity, manufacturability, and ease of operation.

Control choice

Electrical gripper control avoids adding a mechanical trigger or linkage around the F/T sensor.

ARCHITECTURES COMPARED
  • HH-2One-Hand Motorized EE + Force/Load-Cell Trigger
  • HH-3Two-Hand Motorized EE
  • HH-4Passive / Fixed Key Holder
  • HH-5One-Hand Unmotorized EE
03 / VALIDATION CONFIGURATION

HH-4 Passive / Fixed Key Holder

Useful for early validation: removing gripper-actuation and key-slip uncertainty lets the team isolate F/T sensing, gravity compensation, pose tracking, synchronization, and key-contact behavior.

CALCULATED STUDY / OPERATOR INTERFACE

Operator Ergonomics & Mass Distribution

ENGINEERING QUESTION

How does component mass distribution affect sustained wrist and forearm loading while an operator holds the handheld system?

Design criteria

PRELIMINARY TARGETS

< 15 [mm]COM offset from grip axis

Method & inputs

Calculated the moment from the F/T sensor, GoPro, and servo motor relative to the grip axis, then estimated a handle shift toward the system center of mass.

DESIGN DECISION

The original wrist moment is already below the preliminary wrist-moment target, but moving the handle toward the calculated system center of mass can further reduce sustained wrist loading without adding mass.

A counterweight could balance the system, but the calculated counterweight would increase total mass by approximately 15–18 [%], making handle repositioning preferable.

Limitations / next validation

The moment calculation considered only the F/T sensor, GoPro, and servo motor. Printed-part mass was excluded; the device was assumed level; external operating forces were not included. Confirm balance with the assembled device and representative use.

SOLIDWORKS FEA / USER-SIDE INTERFACE

User-Side Structural & F/T Interface Integrity

ENGINEERING QUESTION

Could operator-applied loads create excessive stress or deformation in the printed PLA handle and handle-to-sensor adapter near the ATI Gamma F/T sensor mounting interface?

Method: SolidWorks FEA of the printed PLA handle and handle-to-sensor adapter under four load cases, comparing a fully fixed face with an eight-hole fixture.

Structural screening criteria

Analytical Sanity Check

A simplified cantilever under a 20 [N] transverse load predicted approximately 3.06 [MPa] bending stress and 0.978 [mm] tip displacement. It checks the order of magnitude; SolidWorks represents the actual geometry and directional stiffness more realistically.

Boundary-condition sensitivity

The fully fixed face is an intentionally stiff limiting model. The eight-hole fixture better represents discrete mounting support, but still idealizes the real bolted joint and may exaggerate local stress around fixed hole surfaces.

DESIGN DECISION

All completed FEA cases remained below the preliminary structural screening limits. The results support continued prototyping, but the real eight-screw joint and actual F/T measurement response still require physical validation.

Limitations / next validation

Structural behavior was analyzed. Actual electrical F/T measurement contamination has not yet been experimentally validated. Test the assembled joint and sensor response under representative operator loads.

MATTHEW HA / PRELIMINARY SUBSYSTEM ANALYSIS

Static Vision & Test-Rig Analysis

View full analysis →
ENGINEERING QUESTION

How can the static vision system provide useful coverage of the key-insertion workspace, and what requirements should guide the interchangeable lock test rig?

Scope & method: The report examines static environmental camera coverage, camera-to-lock geometry, and nominal image density. It also develops an interchangeable lock/test-rig concept, tipping-stability design relationships, and material-cost planning.

Camera analysis inputs

PRELIMINARY ANALYSIS
0.55 [m]Analyzed camera-to-lock distance
69.4 [°]D435i RGB horizontal FoV
42.5 [°]D435i RGB vertical FoV
1920 × 1080 [pixels]RGB resolution
CALCULATED PRELIMINARY RESULTS0.762 × 0.428 [m] visible area · 2.52 [pixels/mm] nominal pixel density

These calculated values exceed the report's proposed preliminary workspace criterion of 0.40 × 0.35 [m] and nominal pixel-density criterion of 2.0 [pixels/mm]. They are not experimentally validated camera performance.

Test-rig concept

A rigid support structure would hold a replaceable lock module with adjustable positioning. Cameras would use separate rigid mounts. Mounting hardware remains under development.

Tipping-stability framework

The report defines stability as resisting moment divided by applied moment and proposes a safety-factor target of ≥ 1.5. This target has not yet been demonstrated.

DESIGN TAKEAWAY

The preliminary camera calculation supports continued layout development; the interchangeable test rig still needs defined geometry, loads, and physical validation.

Limitations / next validation

Final rig mass, base width, applied insertion force, load height, and finalized material cost remain unresolved. Test-rig stability and material cost are pending design validation.

Current system context: Robotic Key now uses two static D435i cameras. This individual report is retained as preliminary subsystem analysis and will be revised as the final two-camera arrangement and test-rig geometry are finalized.
